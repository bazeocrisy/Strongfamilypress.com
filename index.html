The problem

Families don't fall apart all at once.

They come apart in small moments. A morning that starts twenty minutes behind. A dinner nobody planned. A tone that lands harder than it should. None of it is a crisis. All of it adds up.

What it looks like now

  • Mornings run on shouting and searching
  • You repeat the same instructions daily
  • Everyone else's request gets answered first
  • The house is quiet but tense
  • You've tried chore charts. They lasted a week.

What becomes possible

  • Mornings that don't depend on your memory
  • Kids who know what they're responsible for
  • Time that belongs to your family first
  • A home that feels settled, not braced
  • Structure that holds after the motivation fades

This isn't a book about fixing a broken family. It's written for parents whose families work on paper and feel exhausting in practice.

The framework

The Seven Steps

Each step clears the way for the next. The full teaching, the worksheets, and the household routines are in the book — here's what each step does.

  1. 01

    Say No

    Situation
    Your calendar keeps filling with other people's priorities.
    Shift
    You learn to decline clearly, without guilt or a long explanation.
    Outcome
    Time comes back before you try to do anything with it.
  2. 02

    Become Less Available

    Situation
    You answer everyone immediately, and your family gets the leftovers.
    Shift
    You set hours the outside world doesn't get to interrupt.
    Outcome
    Your attention stops leaking out of the house.
  3. 03

    Build the System

    Situation
    Lists and chore charts collapse within a week.
    Shift
    You replace daily decisions with routines that repeat on their own.
    Outcome
    The week is prepared before it starts, and mornings stop depending on you.
  4. 04

    Rebuild Yourself

    Situation
    You've disappeared into the role. Health, focus, and your relationship slid.
    Shift
    The things that keep you steady move back onto the calendar.
    Outcome
    The person running the home is intact enough to run it.
  5. 05

    Reset the Emotional Temperature

    Situation
    Nobody is fighting, but the house feels tight and everyone can tell.
    Shift
    You learn to read the room you created — and to set it on purpose.
    Outcome
    Children stop bracing and start talking again.

    Every home has a temperature. Children can't name it. They can always feel it.

    Cold & distant Settled Tense & reactive
  6. 06

    Speak With Purpose

    Situation
    You repeat yourself, and corrections turn into arguments.
    Shift
    Slower pace, one instruction at a time, reassurance at the end.
    Outcome
    They hear the message instead of the tone it arrived in.
  7. 07

    Live the Legacy

    Situation
    Your habits are being copied whether or not you chose them.
    Shift
    You decide, on purpose, what gets passed down.
    Outcome
    Your children inherit structure instead of survival.

Why the order matters

Most people start at Step 3 — and that's why it fails.

Nearly every parent who has abandoned a chore chart started with the system. But a system needs room to live in, and a household with no margin has none. Steps 1 and 2 clear the space. Step 3 fills it.

The first three steps work from the outside in — your calendar, your phone, your week. The next three work from the inside out — your health, the tone of your home, the way you speak. The seventh step is what all of it is for.

Tasks change day by day. Systems run every day.

The author

Christopher L. Bazemore I.

[AUTHOR PHOTO NEEDED]

Christopher spent three and a half years in the United States Navy aboard the aircraft carrier USS Constellation, where structure wasn't a preference — it was how everything stayed afloat. He went on to earn an MBA and build a career managing systems and infrastructure in corporate IT.

Then he became a husband, and a father to two children eighteen months apart. School started. The mornings got loud. And he found that the discipline that ran a flight deck and a server room didn't automatically run a household — it had to be rebuilt for one.

This book is the result. It was written from the practical work of keeping a home steady while two parents worked, not from theory.

He lives in Georgia with his wife, Stephanie, and their two children.
